# Whimsical presets: small melodies and human warmth

Research date: 7 September 2026. The brief is a gentler, playful companion to the cinematic presets, drawing on the intimacy of *Her*. Firsthand accounts guide the emotional direction; the synthesis and procedural phrases below are Stillroom's own design choices.

## Four useful observations

### Give the foreground an intimate voice

Owen Pallett recalls that the early score emphasized science fiction, before the film developed into a romance. The revised direction included strings and electric guitars. That account makes the emotional scale more useful than treating *Her* as a generic futuristic synth reference. [Pallett interviewed by *The Berliner*, 22 May 2014](https://www.the-berliner.com/film/a-chat-with-owen-pallett/).

**Our adaptation:** Bring soft, keyed details forward and reduce the low foundation and ambient texture beneath them. Warm pads can hold the room together while individual notes remain easy to follow. Paper lanterns should feel like a small musical thought unfolding nearby, with the broad cinematic brass of Neon skyline remaining a separate choice.

### Make a phrase sound discovered

In his SPIN interview, Pallett describes reshaping the photograph-scene piano cue into a rhapsodic piece, with a performance suggesting a computer improvising. This documents a compositional and performance intention, not a specific generative algorithm. [Pallett interviewed by Harley Brown, *SPIN*, 26 February 2014](https://www.spinmagazine.com/2014/02/owen-pallett-academy-award-oscar-nominee-her-spike-jonze/).

**Our adaptation:** Use short, recognizable contours with small changes of register, timing, and emphasis. Let figures pause before answering themselves. A lilting internal rhythm can make Daydream feel buoyant without requiring a drum pattern. Variation should retain a phrase's identity instead of turning every note into an unrelated surprise.

### Keep the synth sympathetic to the melody

In the same interview, Pallett says Jonze removed many synthetic flourishes to favor the human emotional element. He identifies the Roland Jupiter-8 as a notable exception heard throughout the score. This supports warm synthesis, but supplies no verified patch settings. [*SPIN* interview](https://www.spinmagazine.com/2014/02/owen-pallett-academy-award-oscar-nominee-her-spike-jonze/).

**Our adaptation:** Support the keyed foreground with soft, slowly wavering chords. Paper lanterns uses a rounded piano-like attack; Daydream explores a warmer bell or music-box impression. The latter is our extension of the brief, not an assertion that the film used a particular music box. The piano-like sound is synthesized, not an acoustic-piano sample or physical model.

### Leave room for tenderness

At KCRW's own session, Karen O describes Jonze's assignment for “The Moon Song” as something the two characters might invent together. The session producer records the performers adjusting the space between parts, and Karen O's fondness for yearning and melancholy. This concerns a song within the film, distinct from the instrumental score. [KCRW session report and interview, 13 February 2014](https://www.kcrw.com/stories/spike-jonze-karen-o-and-kk-barrett-play-the-moon-song-live-on-kcrw).

**Our adaptation:** Keep the whimsy gentle: audible rests, restrained accents, rounded transients, and some wistfulness in the harmony. Optional Pulse can add movement when requested, but the default melody should already have a relaxed sense of motion.

## Listening criteria

Both presets use original generated notes and synthesis. Check that details stay distinct through the room, remain soft at higher Density, and retain character in mono. Sustained notes must survive ordinary control changes. These additions should complement Distant tide without changing its established sound or saved variations.
